# ADR-002: Domain Adaptation Strategy

**Status**: Accepted
**Date**: 2026-01-15
**Deciders**: ML platform team

---

## Context

The base Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model has broad general capability but limited knowledge of
enterprise-specific terminology, internal procedures, and proprietary technical documentation.
Domain adaptation is needed to improve faithfulness (answers grounded in retrieved documents)
and to reduce hallucination of internal product names, acronyms, and process steps.

Several adaptation strategies were evaluated:

| Strategy | Training required | Data required | Inference cost | Forgetting risk |
|----------|------------------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|
| RAG alone | None | Retrieval corpus | Baseline | None |
| RAFT fine-tuning | Yes (LoRA) | Q&A from docs | Baseline + adapter | Low (LoRA) |
| InstructLab | Yes (LAB method) | Taxonomy YAML | Baseline + adapter | Low (LoRA) |
| Full fine-tuning | Yes (full weights) | Large labeled set | Baseline | High |

Requirements for the adaptation strategy:

- Must not degrade general reasoning capability (catastrophic forgetting is unacceptable)
- Must be feasible on a single A100 80GB GPU within the OpenShift AI cluster
- Must be reproducible: training runs tracked in MLflow, datasets versioned with DVC
- Must support incremental updates as the document corpus evolves
- Must maintain or improve faithfulness scores (RAGAS `faithfulness >= 0.85`)

---

## Decision

**Layered approach**: RAG + reranking as the baseline for all queries. RAFT fine-tuning for
high-value domains where faithfulness is critical. InstructLab for community alignment and
Red Hat product vocabulary. Full fine-tuning is explicitly avoided.

### Layer 1: RAG + Reranking (all queries)

The retrieval-augmented generation pipeline with BGE cross-encoder reranking is the foundation
for every query. This alone resolves the majority of faithfulness issues by ensuring the LLM
has relevant grounding context. No training is required. This is the baseline against which all
adaptation strategies are measured (see `evaluation/ragas_comparison.md`).

### Layer 2: RAFT Fine-Tuning (high-value domains)

Retrieval-Augmented Fine-Tuning (RAFT, Gorur et al. 2024) trains the model to distinguish
relevant from distractor retrieved chunks and to synthesise answers citing specific passages.
For domains where faithfulness failures have high business cost (compliance, legal, financial
procedures), a RAFT adapter is trained on a Q&A dataset generated from the domain corpus.

Training configuration:
- Method: QLoRA (4-bit quantisation, LoRA rank 16, alpha 32)
- Framework: TRL `SFTTrainer` with Axolotl config
- Dataset: generated by the `evals/raft_dataset_generator.py` script, versioned with DVC
- Hardware: single A100 80GB, approximately 4 hours per domain adapter
- Serving: LoRA adapter loaded into vLLM via `--lora-modules`

Adapters are tracked in the MLflow Model Registry. Only adapters tagged `production` are loaded
by the vLLM Deployment.

### Layer 3: InstructLab (Red Hat / community alignment)

InstructLab's LAB (Large-scale Alignment for chatBots) method uses a taxonomy-driven synthetic
data generation workflow to teach the model Red Hat product vocabulary, OpenShift-specific
procedures, and community norms. This adapter is trained separately from the RAFT adapters and
loaded as a secondary adapter on the vLLM instance.

InstructLab is chosen here because:
- It is the Red Hat-endorsed approach for aligning models with Red Hat ecosystem knowledge
- The taxonomy YAML format is version-controlled and human-readable, enabling domain experts to
  contribute training signal without ML expertise
- It produces LoRA adapters that compose with RAFT adapters at inference time

### Full Fine-Tuning: Not Selected

Full fine-tuning of all model weights is explicitly ruled out for the following reasons:

- **Catastrophic forgetting**: full fine-tuning on a domain corpus degrades general reasoning,
  instruction-following, and safety alignment. Quantifying and mitigating this requires extensive
  evaluation across capability benchmarks, which is beyond the current team capacity.
- **Cost**: full fine-tuning of a 8B model requires multiple A100 GPUs for multiple days.
  LoRA adapters achieve comparable domain improvement at 1/10th the compute cost.
- **Inflexibility**: a fully fine-tuned checkpoint cannot be hot-swapped. Any model update
  requires a full redeployment. LoRA adapters can be swapped on a running vLLM instance.
- **Regulatory risk**: a heavily fine-tuned model's behaviour is harder to audit against the
  base model's safety alignment. The platform operates in a regulated enterprise context where
  auditability is required.

---

## Consequences

### What becomes easier

- Adapters can be updated independently of the base model. When a new version of
  Llama-3.1 is released, existing LoRA adapters can be re-evaluated and often reused.
- The MLflow Model Registry provides a clear audit trail: every adapter version, its training
  dataset (DVC hash), evaluation metrics, and promotion history are recorded.
- Domain experts can contribute to InstructLab taxonomy without writing training code.
- The RAFT adapter training pipeline is fully automated via KFP; adding a new domain requires
  only a new corpus bucket and a pipeline parameter change.

### What becomes harder

- Managing multiple LoRA adapters (one per high-value domain, plus the InstructLab adapter)
  increases the model versioning surface. A clear naming convention and registry policy are
  required.
- RAFT dataset generation quality directly determines adapter quality. Poor Q&A generation
  (hallucinated answers in the training set) can degrade faithfulness rather than improving it.
  The generation pipeline must include a filtering step that removes low-confidence examples.
- Adapter compatibility must be verified whenever the base model is updated. A regression test
  suite (RAGAS evaluation over a held-out set) must pass before any adapter is promoted to
  production.
- Teams unfamiliar with LoRA may not intuitively understand that the adapter and base model
  must match in architecture. Documentation and CI checks are needed to prevent mismatches.
